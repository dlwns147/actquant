\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language model (LLM) inference is increasingly constrained by two
different forms of memory.  Model weights constitute a large, static footprint,
whereas the key--value (KV) cache grows with the batch size and context length.
Weight-only quantization reduces the former and can accelerate memory-bound
linear layers~\citep{frantar2023gptq,lin2024awq}; KV-cache quantization and
channel pruning reduce the latter and are especially important for long-context
serving~\citep{liu2024kivi,xu2025think}.  Treating these components separately,
however, leaves a central deployment question unanswered: given one end-to-end
memory budget, how should precision be distributed across weight modules, KV
layers, K/V quantization parameters, and retained KV channels?

This is a combinatorial, multi-objective problem.  In the current search space
for Llama-3.1-8B, each of 224 weight matrices chooses among 2-, 3-, and 4-bit
formats.  Each layer additionally chooses K and V bit/group-size pairs and K/V
retained dimensions.  The resulting space contains approximately
$3^{224}7^{64}5^{64}\approx 5\times10^{205}$ configurations.  Evaluating even a
small fraction is impossible.  More importantly, the cost of a configuration is
heterogeneous.  An activation-independent quantizer such as HQQ can assemble a
mixed-precision model from precomputed weight banks~\citep{badri2023hqq}, but an
activation-aware method such as AWQ depends on calibration activations and must
rebuild the quantized weights for a new layer-wise allocation~\citep{lin2024awq}.
In our implementation, a fresh Llama-3.1-8B AWQ build and evaluation takes about
eight minutes on one GPU, which makes the initial design size---and therefore
surrogate quality---a first-order concern.

AMQ recently made layer-wise mixed-precision \emph{weight-only} search practical
through search-space pruning, an HQQ quantization proxy, a learned quality
predictor, and iterative multi-objective search~\citep{lee2025amq}.  Its proxy
argument is deliberately strong: if the proxy and deployment quantizers induce
the same total order over configurations, their Pareto frontiers coincide.  The
joint weight--KV setting exposes a boundary of this argument.  A proxy can
preserve the coarse order of weight configurations when the KV cache is fixed,
yet disagree with the deployment method about which \emph{weight placement} is
best after the KV configuration changes.  Thus, ``the proxy correlates with the
deployment method'' is not a sufficient statement.  We must distinguish
cross-budget order, which is useful for restricting the space, from
within-budget order, which determines the final architecture.

Our key observation is that the joint problem naturally separates into these
two questions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Where should the search look?}  If per-axis order is stable
    under changes to the partner axis, the Cartesian product of per-axis Pareto
    sets covers the useful part of the joint space.
    \item \textbf{Which configuration should it select?}  Per-axis observations
    do not identify the values, exchange rate, or local ordering in the interior
    of that product.  These must be learned from joint measurements under the
    deployment quantizer.
\end{enumerate}

This order--value distinction motivates \method.  Stage~1 fixes the partner axes
at high precision and independently searches (i) layer-wise weight precision and
(ii) effective KV precision, where the latter combines K/V bit-width, group size,
and channel pruning.  It uses HQQ weight banks, output-distribution divergence,
and iterative surrogate-assisted multi-objective search.  Stage~2 constructs
candidate architectures from $\epsilon$-Pareto blocks returned by Stage~1,
allows limited mutations at contested layer/module positions, and performs a
joint search using AWQ measurements.  The search therefore spends expensive
activation-aware builds only in a much smaller, structured region.

The restriction is supported by a simple but load-bearing theorem.  For each
axis, we directly define the largest deployment-loss increase caused by replacing
a configuration with one that Stage~1 ranks no worse.  If these audited violation
margins are $V_W$ and $V_{KV}$, every joint architecture is cost-dominated by an
architecture in the product of the two fronts with at most
$V_W+V_{KV}$ additional loss.  The theorem needs cost separability and adequate
per-axis fronts, but not loss additivity.  Limited mutations only enlarge the
candidate set, so the coverage statement is retained.

The theorem also clarifies where a proxy can be used.  A monotone transfer from
HQQ loss to AWQ loss preserves a per-axis Pareto set.  In contrast, a rank
reversal between two equal-cost joint configurations is a direct certificate
that proxy selection can incur positive AWQ regret.  Preliminary measurements
exhibit exactly this split: HQQ-to-AWQ Spearman correlation is 1.00 on the two
individual axes, but falls to 0.40 among fine-grained candidates in the same
budget cells, with 29\% pairwise discordance.  Proxy information is therefore
well suited to Stage~1 and to feature learning, but not to the final Stage~2
labels.

Two mechanisms make AWQ-labeled search affordable.  First, we fit
proxy-supervised partial least-squares (PLS) projections separately to one-hot
weight and KV genomes, reducing the current discrete encoding to 16 latent
coordinates plus two exact cost coordinates.  An AWQ-trained surrogate operates
in this 18-dimensional space.  Second, the AWQ build depends only on the weight
allocation.  For each selected weight anchor we evaluate several diverse KV
companions by swapping only the KV configuration, amortizing one weight build
over many joint labels.

Our contributions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item We formulate joint weight--KV compression as a two-stage,
    three-objective search and separate the ordinal role of per-axis search from
    the cardinal role of joint measurement.
    \item We give an auditable front-product coverage theorem for exact and
    $\epsilon$-Pareto per-axis sets, together with a sufficient approximate
    sufficiency condition and an explicit statement of its empirical scope.
    \item We establish a proxy-safety criterion: monotone transfer preserves a
    frontier, while equal-budget rank reversals can change the selected
    architecture.  This explains why HQQ can guide Stage~1 but AWQ information
    is required in Stage~2.
    \item We introduce proxy-supervised PLS reduction and weight-amortized
    multi-KV evaluation to improve the number and usefulness of AWQ labels per
    expensive build.
    \item We provide paired-grid and within-budget audit protocols that measure
    the assumptions required by the theory rather than relying only on global
    correlation.
\end{itemize}

The present draft intentionally limits claims to the audited configuration
domain.  In particular, the current $20\times20$ audit uses blocks sampled from
the discovered fronts; off-front mutations, Stage~1 front error, cross-model
transfer, and the optimal number of KV companions remain explicit experimental
questions rather than hidden assumptions.
