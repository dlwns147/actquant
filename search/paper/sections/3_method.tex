\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_W\times\mathcal{X}_{KV}$ be a finite configuration
space.  A weight configuration $w\in\mathcal{X}_W$ assigns a bit-width to every
linear module.  A cache configuration $k\in\mathcal{X}_{KV}$ assigns, per layer,
K/V bit-widths and group sizes and K/V channel-pruning dimensions.  We use
$a=(w,k)$ for a joint architecture.

For quantization method $M$, quality loss is the Jensen--Shannon divergence
between the output distributions of the full-precision teacher and the
compressed model,
\begin{equation}
  y_M(a)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|}
  \sum_{(x,t)\in\mathcal{D}}
  \jsd\!\left(p_{\mathrm{FP}}(\cdot\mid x,t),
              p_{M,a}(\cdot\mid x,t)\right),
  \label{eq:jsd}
\end{equation}
where lower is better.  The calibration protocol is held fixed across all
comparisons.  Our current search protocol uses 128 WikiText-2 samples of length
2048, evaluates the final 512 tokens through the real cache path, and advances
with stride 128.  These details are part of the objective definition: changing
the context or cache path can change the measured W--KV exchange rate.

The two cost coordinates are
\begin{equation}
  c(a)=\big(c_W(w),c_{KV}(k;T)\big),
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $c_W$ is a parameter-count-weighted average weight precision and $c_{KV}$
is either effective KV precision or exact cache memory at context length $T$,
depending on the reporting target.  Exact memory accounting includes
quantization metadata, retained channel dimension,
the full-precision residual window, and any fixed attention-sink window.  By
construction, $c_W$ depends only on $w$ and $c_{KV}$ only on $k$.  This exact cost
separability will matter; we make no analogous assumption about $y_M$.

The deployment objective is the three-objective minimization
\begin{equation}
  \min_{(w,k)\in\mathcal{X}}
  \left(y_{\deploy}(w,k),c_W(w),c_{KV}(k;T)\right).
  \label{eq:moo}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, a deployment can choose the measured architecture with minimum
$y_{\deploy}$ under upper bounds on the two cost coordinates.

\subsection{Two-stage search overview}
\label{sec:overview}

Directly optimizing Eq.~\eqref{eq:moo} is both combinatorial and build-bound.
\method{} decomposes it as follows.

\paragraph{Stage 1: per-axis order.}
Choose high-precision reference partners $r_W$ and $r_{KV}$.  With the cheap
proxy method $P=\proxy$, define
\begin{equation}
  z_W(w)=y_P(w,r_{KV}),\qquad
  z_{KV}(k)=y_P(r_W,k).
  \label{eq:axis_scores}
\end{equation}
We independently search $(z_W,c_W)$ and $(z_{KV},c_{KV})$ using an initial
design, an iteratively refit surrogate, and NSGA-II candidate generation.  Each
search returns an archive and an $\eta_i$-Pareto band rather than only one point
per cost.  The band is structurally diversified so that nearly tied allocations
are not collapsed to a single layer pattern.

\paragraph{Stage 2: joint values.}
We form a candidate space from the Cartesian product of weight and KV blocks in
the two bands.  Axis-block crossover retains coherent structures found in
Stage~1.  Mutation resamples values preferentially at positions whose Stage~1
front blocks disagree; positions on which at least 95\% of the blocks agree can
be frozen.  This product-plus-mutation space is optimized for
$(y_{\deploy},c_W,c_{KV})$ with NSGA-III.  A surrogate proposes candidates, but
only measured AWQ values enter the final archive and final budgeted selection.

At each iteration, Stage~2 performs the following operations:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item fit the AWQ-loss surrogate on the current archive using the
    proxy-supervised embedding in Sec.~\ref{sec:pls};
    \item generate product, crossover, and mutation candidates with NSGA-III;
    \item select weight anchors that cover the current
    $(c_W,c_{KV})$ Pareto geometry, then attach $K-1$ diverse KV companions to
    each anchor;
    \item build AWQ weights once per distinct weight anchor, evaluate all of its
    KV companions, and append every successful measurement to the archive.
\end{enumerate}

This design gives Stage~1 an ordinal role---identify useful blocks---and Stage~2
a cardinal role---learn the loss surface and resolve allocations under a joint
budget.

\subsection{Why the product of per-axis fronts is enough}
\label{sec:coverage}

We next formalize the weakest quantity required by the restriction.  The
statement is method-aware: Stage~1 ordering may come from HQQ while the joint
loss in the conclusion is measured by AWQ.

\begin{definition}[$\eta$-front coverage]
A set $\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_i\subseteq\mathcal{X}_i$ is an $\eta_i$-cover of
axis $i$ if, for every $x_i\in\mathcal{X}_i$, there exists
$p_i\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_i$ such that
\begin{equation}
  c_i(p_i)\le c_i(x_i),\qquad
  z_i(p_i)\le z_i(x_i)+\eta_i.
  \label{eq:eta_cover}
\end{equation}
For an exact finite-space Pareto set, $\eta_i=0$.  For a discovered archive,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta_cover} is an empirical adequacy condition.
\end{definition}

For a replacement on axis $i$, define the conditional ordering-violation
margin
\begin{equation}
 V_i(\eta_i)=
 \sup\!\left\{
 \left[y_D(u,x_{-i})-y_D(u',x_{-i})\right]_+:
 \begin{array}{l}
 c_i(u)\le c_i(u'),\\[-2pt]
 z_i(u)\le z_i(u')+\eta_i,\\[-2pt]
 x_{-i}\in\mathcal{X}_{-i}
 \end{array}\right\},
 \label{eq:violation}
\end{equation}
where $D=\deploy$ and $[q]_+=\max(q,0)$.  This is the largest amount by which a
replacement that Stage~1 regards as no worse can hurt deployment quality under
an arbitrary partner configuration.

\begin{theorem}[Front-product coverage]
\label{thm:coverage}
Assume the cost map is separable as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost}, and each
$\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_i$ is an $\eta_i$-cover.  For every
$a\in\mathcal{X}$ there exists
$b\in\mathcal{C}=\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_W\times
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{KV}$ such that
\begin{equation}
  c(b)\le c(a)\quad\text{componentwise},\qquad
  y_D(b)\le y_D(a)+V_W(\eta_W)+V_{KV}(\eta_{KV}).
  \label{eq:coverage_bound}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the product set weakly cost-dominates and
$\epsilon$-quality-dominates the joint Pareto set for
$\epsilon=V_W+V_{KV}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $p_W$ and $p_{KV}$ be the covering points from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta_cover}, and set $b=(p_W,p_{KV})$.  Cost separability gives
$c(b)\le c(a)$.  Replace one axis at a time.  By
Eq.~\eqref{eq:violation},
\[
 y_D(p_W,p_{KV})
 \le y_D(a_W,p_{KV})+V_W(\eta_W)
 \le y_D(a_W,a_{KV})+V_W(\eta_W)+V_{KV}(\eta_{KV}).
\]
\end{proof}

The theorem does not say that W and KV losses are additive, nor that interaction
is small.  It only charges interaction components that reverse a Stage~1 order.
A saturation effect can change absolute values substantially while leaving
$V_i$ small.  Moreover, mutations do not invalidate the result if the searched
space retains $\mathcal{C}$: they expand the candidate set and can only improve
its best attainable point.

A more interpretable, but stronger, sufficient condition connects the theorem
to approximate sufficient dimension reduction.

\begin{corollary}[Monotone sufficient summaries]
\label{cor:sufficiency}
Suppose there is a componentwise nondecreasing function $F$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \left|y_D(w,k)-F(z_W(w),z_{KV}(k))\right|\le\epsilon_0
 \quad\text{for all }(w,k)\in\mathcal{X},
 \label{eq:sufficiency}
\end{equation}
and $F$ is coordinate-wise Lipschitz with constants $L_W,L_{KV}$.  Then the
point $b$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} satisfies
\begin{equation}
 y_D(b)\le y_D(a)+2\epsilon_0+L_W\eta_W+L_{KV}\eta_{KV}.
 \label{eq:sufficient_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:sufficiency} at $a$ and $b$, monotonicity to the covering
relations, and the Lipschitz bounds to the $\eta_i$ slack.  The residual is paid
only at the two endpoints, giving $2\epsilon_0$ rather than one term per axis.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Empirical audit.}
We stratified 20 weight blocks and 20 effective-KV blocks from the Stage~1
fronts and measured all 400 combinations with AWQ.  At the current stride-128
protocol, partner-conditioned rankings have minimum Kendall $\tau=0.942$; the
corresponding empirical margins are
$\widehat V_W=0.0103$ and $\widehat V_{KV}=0.0034$, totaling 0.0137 (2.6\% of
the observed JSD range).  These values are stronger evidence than correlation
alone because Eq.~\eqref{eq:coverage_bound} depends on the largest harmful
reversal, not the average order.

The scope is important.  The 400 cells audit on-front blocks.  A mutation can
create an off-front block that plays both the replaced and partner roles in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:violation}; those roles have not yet been exhaustively audited.
Similarly, archive-front adequacy in Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta_cover} is not guaranteed
by a single search run.  We therefore treat the current numbers as an empirical
instance on the audited block product.  Section~\ref{sec:analysis_protocol}
specifies the additional falsification tests needed for a broader claim.

\subsection{Where can we use a quantization proxy?}
\label{sec:proxy}

Let $y_P$ and $y_D$ be proxy (HQQ) and deployment (AWQ) losses under identical
cost accounting.  AMQ's proxy theorem can be written as follows.

\begin{theorem}[Pareto invariance under order equivalence]
\label{thm:proxy_invariance}
If, for all $a,b\in\mathcal{X}$,
\begin{equation}
  y_P(a)<y_P(b)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_D(a)<y_D(b),
  \label{eq:order_equiv}
\end{equation}
then $\pf(y_P,c)=\pf(y_D,c)$.  A sufficient condition is
$y_D=T\!\circ y_P$ for a strictly increasing $T$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $b$ dominates $a$ under $(y_P,c)$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:order_equiv} makes the same
quality comparison under $y_D$, and conversely.  Thus the dominance relations,
and hence the nondominated sets, coincide.
\end{proof}

The condition is global.  High global Spearman correlation, or monotone transfer
on each isolated axis, does not establish it in the joint interior.  The next
proposition gives a local certificate of failure.

\begin{proposition}[Equal-budget reversal certificate]
\label{prop:reversal}
Let $a,b$ be two configurations with $c(a)=c(b)$.  If
\begin{equation}
 y_P(a)<y_P(b)\quad\text{and}\quad y_D(a)>y_D(b),
 \label{eq:reversal}
\end{equation}
then proxy selection over the feasible pair $\{a,b\}$ incurs deployment regret
$y_D(a)-y_D(b)>0$.  Furthermore, if $a$ is proxy-nondominated in the full space,
the proxy and deployment Pareto sets differ because $b$ deployment-dominates
$a$.
\end{proposition}

This elementary certificate is operationally useful: it says exactly which
correlation must be audited.  Cross-budget correlation is dominated by the
large effect of changing the total number of bits.  Final selection instead
depends on rank inside a narrow cost cell, where two architectures spend the
same resources at different layers.

Our preliminary measurements reveal two regimes.  On the Stage~1 weight and KV
axes, HQQ-to-AWQ transfer is monotone over the audited front blocks (Spearman
$\rho=1.000$ for both axes); fitted transfer slopes are 0.705 for weights and
0.970 for KV.  Hence HQQ is a useful source of per-axis order, although it does
not reproduce AWQ values.  On W-placement slices with matched average weight and
effective-KV costs, however, the mean within-cell correlation falls to 0.40 and
the pairwise discordance rate is 29\%.  Thus one scalar increasing transfer
$T$ cannot explain the joint space, and Theorem~\ref{thm:proxy_invariance}
cannot justify proxy-only Stage~2 selection.

This leads to a precise division of labor.  HQQ is used (i) to search the two
axes, (ii) to choose front blocks, and (iii) to learn a low-dimensional
representation.  AWQ labels are used to fit the joint surrogate, update the
Stage~2 archive, and select the final architecture.  This does not imply that
every joint candidate needs an independent AWQ build: a small amount of AWQ
information can train a correction model, and Sec.~\ref{sec:multi_kv} amortizes
one build across multiple candidates.  The defensible claim is that
\emph{deployment-method information} is required in the joint interior, not
that exhaustive deployment-method search is required.

\subsection{Proxy-supervised surrogate embedding}
\label{sec:pls}

The current discrete genome has 352 cells: 224 weight cells and 128 KV bit/group
and pruning cells.  A direct ordinal encoding imposes an artificial linear
geometry---for example, the error jump from 3 to 2 bits need not equal the jump
from 4 to 3 bits.  A full one-hot encoding removes this artifact but expands the
weight and KV halves to 672 and 896 coordinates, respectively.  Either input is
high-dimensional relative to an AWQ initial design of roughly 100 builds.

We use the large Stage~1 HQQ archives to learn a supervised representation.
Let $h_W(w)$ and $h_{KV}(k)$ be per-cell one-hot encodings.  Separately for each
axis, PLS finds a projection whose coordinates have high covariance with the
square root of the Stage~1 JSD:
\begin{align}
 R_W &= \operatorname{PLS}_8\!\left(h_W(w),\sqrt{z_W(w)}\right),\\
 R_{KV} &= \operatorname{PLS}_8\!\left(h_{KV}(k),\sqrt{z_{KV}(k)}\right).
 \label{eq:pls}
\end{align}
The joint surrogate input is
\begin{equation}
 \phi(w,k)=\left[R_W^\top h_W(w),
                   R_{KV}^\top h_{KV}(k),
                   c_W(w),c_{KV}(k)\right]\in\R^{18}.
 \label{eq:embedding}
\end{equation}
An ARD Gaussian process with a Mat\'ern-$3/2$ kernel predicts
$\sqrt{y_D}$ from $\phi$.  HQQ therefore supplies the representation, while the
regression head and every value used for selection are learned from AWQ.

Eight components per axis are the current default.  On held-out Stage~1 data,
the proxy-supervision $R^2$ reaches 0.983 for weights and 0.992 for KV at eight
components; the KV score decreases slightly at 12--16 components.  On the
current AWQ archive, the production 18-dimensional pipeline obtains mean
within-cell Spearman correlation $0.640\pm0.054$ with 100 training samples and
$0.700\pm0.008$ with 430, while budget-band correlation is 0.955 and 0.970.
These are sanity checks, not yet a clean causal demonstration that PLS improves
sample efficiency: the final comparison must hold the test set, training size,
and surrogate head fixed.

Our planned ablation therefore compares raw ordinal, one-hot, PCA, PLS with
$d\in\{2,4,8,16\}$, and archive-only self-PLS inputs under the same head and
fixed held-out candidates.  Training sizes are
$N\in\{25,50,100,200,400\}$, with splits grouped by weight allocation to prevent
KV companions from leaking the same AWQ build across train and test.  We report
global and within-budget Spearman correlation, RMSE, top-1 budgeted regret, and
Pareto hypervolume.  This design tests the actual claim---more useful AWQ
decisions per label---rather than only reconstruction quality of the PLS space.

\subsection{Multi-KV configuration evaluation}
\label{sec:multi_kv}

AWQ calibration and weight construction depend on $w$ but not on the selected
KV-cache configuration.  After building $M_{\deploy,w}$ once, we can replace the
cache implementation and evaluate
\begin{equation}
 (w,k_1),(w,k_2),\ldots,(w,k_K)
 \label{eq:kv_bundle}
\end{equation}
without rebuilding the weights.  If a weight build costs $t_W$ and one KV swap
and loss evaluation costs $t_E$, $B$ weight anchors cost
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathrm{bundle}}=B(t_W+Kt_E),
 \quad\text{versus}\quad
 T_{\mathrm{naive}}=BK(t_W+t_E).
 \label{eq:amortization}
\end{equation}
The saving is $B(K-1)t_W$.  A GPU correctness test on the current implementation
measured identical JSD for a KV-swapped model and an independently rebuilt model;
the first member took about 492 seconds and the reused member completed below the
one-second logging resolution.

For each selected weight anchor, we choose KV companions from the Stage~1 KV
pool.  Candidates are first filtered by the predicted three-objective Pareto set
and then selected to cover the $(c_W,c_{KV})$ plane relative to the measured
archive.  We force the low- and high-KV endpoints when necessary because a flat
surrogate tail can otherwise remove the high-capacity endpoint through
dominance, leaving the surrogate uncalibrated exactly at a budget boundary.

Multi-KV evaluation increases label count per build but creates clustered data.
Large $K$ densely resolves the KV response for a few weight allocations; small
$K$ explores more independent weight allocations if total labels or wall time
is fixed.  Consequently, reporting only ``architectures evaluated'' exaggerates
the effective sample size.  We define $K$ as the \emph{total} configurations per
weight build ($K=1$ means no companion) and propose a three-view ablation:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Fixed build budget:} hold the number of AWQ weight anchors
    $B$ constant and sweep $K\in\{1,5,10,20,40\}$.  This measures the marginal
    value of cheap KV labels per expensive build.
    \item \textbf{Fixed label budget:} hold $BK$ constant.  This exposes the
    trade-off between independent W coverage and within-W KV resolution.
    \item \textbf{Fixed wall clock:} allocate the same GPU-hours using measured
    $t_W$ and $t_E$.  This is the deployment-relevant comparison when KV
    evaluation is no longer negligible at longer contexts.
\end{enumerate}

At checkpoints of 25, 50, 100, and 200 distinct weight builds, we measure
hypervolume and inverted generational distance to the pooled union front,
coverage of an $8\times8$ budget grid, within-cell surrogate rank correlation,
final budgeted top-1 regret, number of distinct W families, and maximum KV gap
within each family.  All metrics are averaged over at least three search seeds,
and predictor validation is grouped by W family.  The current implementation
uses $K=11$ in this notation (one anchor plus ten companions); we treat it as a
working default, not an optimum, until this ablation is complete.

\subsection{Assumption audits and falsification protocol}
\label{sec:analysis_protocol}

The theoretical claims above are conditional, so the empirical section should
be organized as an assumption audit rather than a collection of global
correlations.  We propose the following minimum protocol.

\paragraph{Paired $20\times20$ rank grid.}
Stratify 20 blocks from each per-axis $\eta$-front by cost, evaluate all 400
cross-products with both HQQ and AWQ, and report partner-conditioned Kendall
$\tau$, Spearman $\rho$, pairwise discordance, and the worst violation margins
$\widehat V_W,\widehat V_{KV}$.  The maximum margin is the statistic connected
to Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage}; $\tau$ alone can hide one harmful reversal.

\paragraph{Off-front swap chains.}
Sample at least 30 off-front or mutated joint architectures $a$.  For each one,
construct its per-axis front projections and measure the three points needed by
the sequential proof: $(a_W,a_{KV})$, $(a_W,p_{KV})$, and
$(p_W,p_{KV})$.  This requires roughly 60 new evaluations because the projected
corner can be reused.  It directly tests the roles that the existing on-front
grid does not cover.

\paragraph{Stage~1 adequacy.}
Repeat each per-axis search with independent seeds and measure mutual domination,
hypervolume gap, and the fraction of one run's front $\eta$-covered by the other.
This estimates the Stage~1 term hidden inside Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta_cover}.  Without
this audit, Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} applies to the true fronts but not
necessarily to the fronts found by the algorithm.

\paragraph{Proxy boundary.}
Report HQQ--AWQ agreement at four resolutions: per-axis, cross-budget, fixed
$(c_W,c_{KV})$ cells, and structurally distant pairs within a cell.  For each
budget, measure the AWQ regret of the HQQ-best architecture and the recall of the
AWQ best under an HQQ top-$q$ shortlist.  Pareto overlap and dominated
hypervolume should accompany correlation; neither global $R^2$ nor a visually
similar frontier establishes Eq.~\eqref{eq:order_equiv}.

\paragraph{Generalization.}
The full audit should be repeated on at least one architecture with a different
attention design (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B) and on a long-context dataset.  The primary
metric remains answer-phase JSD for search efficiency, but final architectures
must be evaluated with perplexity and long-context tasks such as LongBench or
RULER.  A search-metric Pareto improvement is not by itself evidence of a
deployment-quality improvement.

\subsection{Limitations of the current guarantee}
\label{sec:limitations}

Theorem~\ref{thm:coverage} is an existence statement, not a convergence theorem
for NSGA-III or the surrogate.  End-to-end regret additionally contains (i)
Stage~1 front error, (ii) the audited ordering-violation margins, (iii) Stage~2
search error relative to the product optimum, and (iv) measurement noise among
near ties.  These terms should be reported separately.  The current evidence is
also concentrated on one model and one JSD protocol.  Finally, a lower-cost
point in the product may fall below the lower edge of a two-sided memory band;
the literal corollary is for upper-bound budgets.  In a two-sided reporting bin,
the theorem guarantees a no-more-expensive approximate replacement, not
necessarily a replacement that remains inside the bin.
