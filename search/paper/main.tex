\title{Stage 1 Supplies the Order, Stage 2 Supplies the Values:\\
Efficient Joint Search for Weight and KV-Cache Compression}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Deploying large language models under a memory budget requires allocating
precision jointly between model weights and the key--value (KV) cache.  The
resulting layer-wise space is too large for direct black-box optimization, and
activation-aware weight quantizers make every new weight allocation expensive
to build.  We present \method, a two-stage search framework that separates the
question of \emph{where to search} from the question of \emph{what to select}.
Stage~1 searches weight and effective-KV axes independently with a cheap
quantization proxy.  Stage~2 searches the product of their $\epsilon$-Pareto
sets, augmented by structure-aware mutations, and obtains labels with the
deployment quantizer.  A front-product coverage theorem shows that this
restriction approximately covers the joint Pareto set whenever partner-induced
ordering violations are small; importantly, the result does not require an
additive loss.  We then characterize the boundary of quantization proxies:
monotone proxy-to-deployment transfer is sufficient for per-axis frontier
preservation, whereas a same-budget rank reversal certifies that proxy-only
joint selection can incur deployment regret.  To make the expensive second
stage sample-efficient, we learn a low-dimensional, proxy-supervised partial
least-squares embedding and amortize each activation-aware weight build over
multiple KV configurations.  Preliminary audits on Llama-3.1-8B support the
ordering assumptions on a paired $20\times20$ grid, while also revealing a
substantial proxy rank collapse among fine-grained allocations at the same
budget.  These findings motivate a principled division of labor: use the proxy
to construct and represent the search space, but use the deployment quantizer
to resolve its joint interior.
\end{abstract}
